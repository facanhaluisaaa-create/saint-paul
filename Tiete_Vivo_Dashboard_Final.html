Is the Tietê Really Recovering?

The headline pollution stain shrank in 2025. The kilometres of good-quality water collapsed. This dashboard separates the two measures so one decision can be made on the right one.

The decision

Should Guarulhos receive priority for targeted pollution monitoring and source investigation before major 2027 investment decisions?

Framed as a question of where to investigate first, not who is responsible. The available data cannot attribute the river's deterioration to any municipality.

The decision-maker

IntegraTietê programme coordinator

São Paulo State Secretariat for the Environment, Infrastructure and Logistics (SEMIL)

Needs: one outcome metric to steer by, a clear reading of 2025 versus 2024, and a defensible next step for 2027 budget planning.

A smaller pollution stain does not necessarily mean the river is recovering.

The stain shrank by 15.9% (207 km → 174 km), yet good-quality water fell by 85 km (119 km → 34 km). Both statements are true at the same time.

Two different measures, not a contradiction

  • Pollution stain (headline): the length of river classified as poor or very poor. In 2025 it is 120 + 54 = 174 km.
  • Good-quality water (North Star): the length of river classified as good. In 2025 it is 34 km.
  • What moved in between: 368 km are now regular. Regular is neither the stain nor good water, so the stain can shrink while good water also shrinks.
  • Why the North Star: it is the environmental outcome the programme actually wants to grow. A stain can shrink without a single extra kilometre becoming good.

So what? — From data to decision

Three insights

Each insight is labelled by the kind of analytics it represents, so the reader knows how much weight it can carry.

Insight 1 · Descriptive
01

The pollution stain decreased from 207 km to 174 km, but good-quality water fell from 119 km to only 34 km. The headline improved while the North Star deteriorated.

What happened. This is a direct reading of the 2024 and 2025 classifications. It is the one fact the coordinator should carry into every 2027 conversation.

Insight 2 · Diagnostic question
02

Regular-quality water increased by 118 km. We need more detailed monitoring, weather and flow information to understand the reasons for this classification shift.

Why it happened is still open. Rainfall, flow, sampling timing and real changes in loads can all move a stretch between classes. The data describe the shift; they do not yet explain it.

Insight 3 · Leading signal
03

Guarulhos's reported sewage-treatment indicator improved from 18% in 2023 to 40% in 2025, suggesting sanitation progress, but this does not establish a causal impact on the monitored river.

A signal worth testing, not a conclusion. Upstream and downstream measurements around Guarulhos tributaries and outfalls are required before any link to river quality can be claimed.

Recommended action · 2027

Prioritize a targeted pollution-monitoring and source-tracing pilot at Guarulhos tributaries and outfalls in 2027.

Collect upstream and downstream measurements, investigate potential pollution sources, and evaluate sanitation improvements before committing major restoration funds.

Use kilometres of good-quality water as a public environmental outcome metric alongside the traditional pollution-stain indicator.

1 · MeasurePaired upstream / downstream sampling at Guarulhos tributaries and outfalls, with flow and rainfall logged.
2 · InvestigateTrace candidate sources from the measurements. No source is named in advance.
3 · DecideEvaluate sanitation progress against river results before releasing major 2027 restoration funds.

Status of this recommendation: an evidence-informed proposal for investigation, not proof of responsibility. The monitoring data do not identify any polluter.

Data limitation

The annual Observando o Tietê monitoring study covers 576 kilometres of a river approximately 1,100 kilometres long. River conditions vary with rainfall, season, and flow. Basin-wide sampling-point percentages and river-kilometre totals use different measurement bases.

The data cannot identify a specific polluter or independently prove the causal impact of sanitation investments.

Coverage: 576 of ~1,100 km. Conditions outside the monitored stretch are unknown.
Two bases: km of river (576-km stretch) vs % of sampling points (basin network). Never mix them in one ratio.
No live data: every number is an annual historical measurement. Nothing on this page is real-time.

The counter has been removed and no live or synthetic sewage volume is generated anywhere on this page. The Tietê-wide figure itself still requires primary-source verification before it is quoted.
